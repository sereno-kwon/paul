Brand

PaulChico, California · Since 1989

Parts made by people
who chose
the hard way.

Paul Component Engineering is a bicycle parts maker that engineer Paul Price started in 1989 in Chico, a small city in Northern California. After a year and a half as an engineer in San Francisco, he quit, moved to Chico and bought a run-down house, chosen above all for a garage big enough for his machines.

His first machines were a mill and a lathe, and the only employee was Paul himself. The company began when an Austrian businessman saw a prototype of his first product, a seatpost quick release, and placed an order. Today, on the site of an old Texaco depot on the edge of Chico, every part is machined in-house on CNC machines and old hand-operated tools.

The Hard Way

Nice parts, lovingly handmade in Chico.

At Paul, raw aluminum stock is cut to rough size, machined on CNC mills, tumbled to finish and packed into a box. When The Radavist visited in 2015, the roughly 2,000 square foot shop was turning out about 200 parts a day, while a prototype machine in the corner milled parts nobody had seen yet.

New parts are tested by riding them in Chico's Bidwell Park. Some of the gnarliest mountain bike trails in the country are a short ride from the shop, and many of the crew ride to work. A day in Chico ends with a group ride on the trails and a dunk in the river.

DesignClean slate

Every part starts from a clean slate. The Klamper took three years to develop, and the caliper body alone went through countless drawing revisions and around 20 prototypes.

MachiningMade in Chico

6061 and 2024 aluminum is machined and finished in-house. Every scrap of aluminum is forged in the USA, and every part is made in the USA.

TestingBidwell Park

The test lab is the trail near the shop. Only parts that Paul and the crew have ridden and refined go on to production.

ServiceFor a lifetime

Sealed cartridge bearings, replaceable pivots and published exploded drawings. With small parts sold separately, these are parts you service and keep for life.

Founder · Paul Price

“Pursuing perfection is what has always mattered. So by default, a traditional, predictable life never did.”

In junior high, Paul was already making skateboards from oak in his parents' garage. Raised by an artist and an engineer, always taking things apart, he came to Chico in 1988 and went to work at Mountain Goat, the shop of legendary Chico framebuilder Jeff Lindsay. His job was grinding fillet-brazed joints, but as Lindsay remembers it, what Paul really wanted to make even then was quick releases.

Even now that the company has grown, Paul stays late in the small personal shop next to the production floor, machining prototypes. It is filled with old manual machines such as a WWII-era shaper and Kearney & Trecker mills, and from the ceiling hang the bikes he built each time a new hub, brake or wheel standard came along.

Each year Paul has hosted Paul Camp, inviting journalists and framebuilders to Chico to tour the shop and ride the trails together. Rather than a flashy trade show booth, he chose to show the place where the parts are made and the trails where they are tested.

02

Disc Brake

KlamperPost mount · Flat mount

No hydraulics.
All the power.

The Klamper is a mechanical disc brake Paul designed from a clean slate. The goal was a brake that is reliable with just a cable and no fluid, easy to adjust and serviceable on the road. After three years of development the first run of 300 was made in Chico in 2015, and today it is Paul's signature part on gravel and touring bikes.

It uses the proven ball-and-ramp principle. Paul added bigger balls and hardened steel contact faces for smoother action and more pad travel. A light start to the lever stroke that firms up and bites hard at the end, with power that is easy to modulate, is the Klamper's character.

View at Rubi Workshop →Specification
01

Bigger balls, harder ramps

Larger balls give smoother action and more pad travel. Where the balls meet the ramps, the steel is hardened.

02

Needle roller bearing

Three hardened steel pins locate the pad actuator, and a flat needle roller thrust bearing takes the load instead of the usual plastic washer.

03

A backing plate with fangs

Fang-shaped reinforcements on the inner backing plate and extra material only where the outer body needs it make for a stiffer caliper.

04

Click-stop pad adjustment

The pad adjusters click into detents located by spring ball plungers. Set the pad gap by hand without tools, and it stays put.

Bed-in

It really bites by the third ride

New pads need a few short rides to bed in. Paul says the Klamper really starts to bite around the third ride. Kool Stop sintered metal pads (KS-D296S) come standard, and it takes SRAM and Avid MTB pattern pads.

Paul does not make rotors. The Klamper works well with Shimano Ice-Tech, SRAM, Avid and most others, though floating rotors with large buttons can rub the caliper. Rubi Workshop will put together the right rotor, adapter and lever combination for you.

03

Rim Brakes

Cantilever · Centerpull · VNeo-Retro, Racer, MiniMoto

Classic shapes,
machined anew.

Since the Stoplight Brakes made in Paul's first Chico machine shop in 1992, brakes have always been what Paul makes most. Even in the disc brake era, Paul has not let go of cantilevers and centerpulls, because steel touring bikes, classic road bikes and 650B randonneurs still look and work best with them.

The Neo-Retro and Racer rework the classic shapes of France's Mafac with modern materials and adjustability. T-section arms resist flex under load, while sealed pivots and adjustable springs keep them smooth and centred for years. All come with salmon Kool Stop Thinline pads.

Racer · Braze-on

A brake chosen together with your framebuilder

The braze-on Racer mounts directly to centerpull bosses that the framebuilder brazes on in a specific position. Boss placement sets the reach for 700C, 650B or 26 inch wheels, and with no bridge it is stiffer and cleaner.

Stock frames use the center mount version, which bolts through the single brake hole above the tire. It suits long-reach classic frames and randonneurs with fenders. In a Rubi Workshop custom build, we decide boss placement and brakes together from the frame stage.

MiniMoto

A V brake for drop bar levers

Ordinary V brakes are designed for long-pull flat bar levers and do not work with road levers. The MiniMoto shortens the arms to match road lever leverage. Unlike a cantilever, there is no separate hanger or straddle wire, and the profile is narrow.

Springs adjust independently left and right, and the pivots run on sealed stainless bushings. There is a quick release for wheel removal, and it clears 44 to 45mm tires without fenders. It is not compatible with Campagnolo levers.

Brake Levers

Canti · Love · Duplex · CrossShort pull, long pull, both

Half of every brake
starts at your fingertips.

A lever and brake only stop properly when the amount of cable they pull, the pull, matches. Cantilevers, centerpulls, the MiniMoto and the short-pull Klamper take short-pull levers; V brakes, the Motolite and the long-pull Klamper take long-pull levers.

Every Paul lever is machined from 6061 aluminum in Chico. Sealed cartridge bearings or stainless pivots make them solid with no play, and large barrel adjusters let you set cable tension by hand.

Stem · Seatpost

Boxcar · Tall and HandsomeMachined from 2024 aluminum

Holding bar and saddle
firmly in place.

The Boxcar stem is machined from 2024 aerospace aluminum. As the name suggests, its rounded box section resists twisting, and the bolts are stainless T25 Torx. Torx was chosen because it is harder to overtighten than a hex key, which protects carbon bars and steerers.

The Tall and Handsome and Tall and Elegant seatposts hold the saddle with two bolts and spherical stainless washers. One screw fine-tunes the angle, and loosening both moves the saddle fore and aft. Tilt range is about 15°, and oval carbon rails are welcome.

Hubs · Drivetrain

Word · FHub · CranksSingle speed since 1996

The hub first made
for single speed mountain bikes.

The Word is a rear hub descended from the first single speed MTB hub Paul made in 1996. Back when there were almost no hubs for people riding mountains without gears, Paul made one with wide flange spacing that allows a dishless wheel build.

The hub body is 6061 and the axle 7075 aluminum. The sealed cartridge bearings are user-adjustable with an adjuster ring, and the Disk Word swaps between quick release, bolt-on and 12mm thru-axle. A hub to ride, service and rebuild for the long haul.

Story

1989 ~ 2026Chico, California

From one lathe in a garage
to a benchmark for American parts.

From a single seatpost quick release to a fundraiser bike built with framebuilders for the 2026 Made Bike Show. While many parts makers moved production overseas, Paul has spent 37 years in Chico designing, machining and riding everything himself.

1988 ~ 2026Drag →
1988

Mountain Goat

Paul quits his engineering job in San Francisco and moves to Chico, where he grinds fillets at Jeff Lindsay's Mountain Goat.

1989

Quick Release

The first product, a seatpost quick release, is made in the garage. A first order from an Austrian businessman launches the company. Wheel quick releases follow.

1992

Stoplight Brakes

The first machine shop opens in Chico. The same year the Stoplight Brakes arrive, and brakes become Paul's signature part.

1996

Word

Paul makes his first hub for single speed mountain bikes, the ancestor of today's Word and Disk Word.

2015

Klamper

After three years of development, the first 300 Klampers are produced. The Radavist spends an afternoon at the Chico shop.

2017

Paul Camp

Paul Camp is held with eleven bikes built by eleven framebuilders around a monster cross and mountain bike theme.

2019

30 Years

Thirty years. A limited green anodized run and a 30th anniversary box set, and seatposts in 30.9 and 31.6mm.

2026

Cascade Cougar
